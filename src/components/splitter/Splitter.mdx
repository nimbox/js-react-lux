# Splitter

Use to lay out panes along a row or a column and let the reader decide how the
space is shared between them. Every adjacent pair gets a divider that can be
dragged, nudged with the keyboard, or double clicked to even the pair out.

<Primary />

## Sizes

Every size carries its own unit, and the unit is the whole of the API:

- **`"3fr"`** — three shares of the space left over. Shares keep their
  proportions as the container resizes.
- **`"240px"`** — 240 pixels, held as the container resizes. The panes measured
  in shares absorb the difference.

That is the only rule. A pane is pinned because its size is in `px`, not because
of a separate flag, and a pane that declares no size takes a single share — which
is what makes an undeclared row an even split.

```tsx
<Splitter direction="horizontal">
    <Splitter.Pane defaultSize="120px"><Navigator /></Splitter.Pane>
    <Splitter.Pane defaultSize="3fr"><Editor /></Splitter.Pane>
    <Splitter.Pane defaultSize="240px"><Inspector /></Splitter.Pane>
</Splitter>
```

Because the unit travels with the value, a size stays readable everywhere it
goes — a change callback, `localStorage`, a controlled array:

```js
onSizesChange(["120px", "1fr", "240px"])
```

Pass `sizes` together with `onSizesChange` to drive the layout yourself, or
`defaultSizes` to set a starting layout positionally. `defaultSizes` wins over
the panes' own `defaultSize`. A `persistenceKey` stores the layout so it survives
a reload; a stored layout that no longer describes the panes — one added,
removed, or switched between `px` and `fr` — is discarded rather than misread.

## Pinned panes

Measuring a pane in pixels is what a sidebar usually wants: an icon rail is 120px
wide because that is how wide icons and labels are, not because it is 12% of the
screen.

| container | pinned | flexible | pinned |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1000px | 120 | 640 | 240 |
| 1600px | 120 | **1240** | 240 |

Any number of panes can be pinned, in any arrangement. A drag only ever moves
the two panes touching that divider; every other pane keeps the pixels it had.

If the pinned panes together ask for more than the container has, they give way
together rather than overflowing it — the flexible panes are squeezed out first,
then the pinned ones shrink in proportion. The squeeze does not change their
stored sizes, so they spring back as soon as the room returns. Keep at least one
pane flexible: with every pane pinned there is nothing to absorb the leftover,
and the layout will not fill its container.

`defaultSize` is a starting width, not a lock: dragging a pinned pane sets a new
pixel width, which then sticks the same way.

## Panes

Each `Splitter.Pane` declares its own limits. `minimumSize` is the smallest size
in pixels a pane may be dragged to, and `collapsible` lets it snap shut when
dragged well past that minimum — dragging back out, or pressing `Enter` on the
divider, restores it.

A pane fills its track and hides its overflow, so give its content a scroll
container if it can grow past the pane.

## Dividers

A divider reserves no space and paints nothing at rest, so the panes touch and
the splitter adds no chrome of its own. Both the grab area — 8px, centred on the
seam — and the 2px bar that fades in on hover, focus and drag float over the
boundary, so revealing the bar never moves the panes.

Two things follow from that. A permanent seam belongs on the pane, as a border,
not on the divider. And because the grab area overlaps each pane's edge by about
4px and sits above it, anything clickable flush against a pane edge will lose to
the divider — give it a little breathing room.

`dividerClassName` styles the visible bar. A `bg-*` there becomes the resting
color; hover, focus and drag still win over it.

```tsx
<Splitter dividerClassName="bg-content-border">
    <Splitter.Pane>…</Splitter.Pane>
    <Splitter.Pane>…</Splitter.Pane>
</Splitter>
```

## Keyboard

The dividers are focusable. Along the splitter's axis, the arrow keys nudge the
boundary, `Home` and `End` drive it to its extremes, `Enter` toggles a
collapsible neighbour, and `Escape` cancels a drag in progress.

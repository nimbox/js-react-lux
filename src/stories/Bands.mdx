# Bands

A band is a region that runs the full width of whatever holds it, separated
from its neighbours by a single line.

The band is the default container and the card is the exception. A card says
"these things are comparable, and there are others like them elsewhere" — a
statistic beside five other statistics, a specimen in a comparison. Most of an
application is not that. A list of rows is not comparable to another list of
rows; it is the only list on the screen, and putting it in a card claims a
relationship that does not exist and costs the width of the padding on both
sides.

There is no `Band` component yet. What follows is the pattern written out in
tokens, and the shape a component would take.

## Three tokens

| Token | Job |
| --- | --- |
| `--color-content-bg` | The data. |
| `--color-content-fill` | Everything that is not the data. |
| `--color-content-border` | Every line between one band and the next. |

A strip carries the fill or it carries nothing. A row declares no background at
all and inherits whichever ground it was put on: border to border that ground is
`content-bg`, and inside a card it is the card's own `content-fg`. The same row
is correct in both without knowing which.

Every separator is `content-border` at one pixel — the same line between two
rows, between a row and a caption, and under the column header. A separator that
changes weight to mean something is a second channel nobody reads.

## Why the fill is not a scale

The fill answers a yes or no: this is not data. Depth is an order. Asking one
channel to carry both is what a `fill-1`, `fill-2` scale would do, and it does
not survive the measurement.

Taking white as L\* 100, the ground is 98.4 and the fill 94.3. A second step
lighter than the fill has about two points to live in, which is invisible — it
was the version of this we started with and could not tell apart. A second step
darker leaves the range where a grey reads as recessed and starts reading as a
colour of its own, at which point it competes with the fill of a selected row,
which has more to say.

Two levels are also all there ever are. Nobody groups a table three deep. So
what has to be distinguished is exactly two captions, and indent with weight
does that with no new token: the outer caption sits at the row's own padding in
`font-semibold text-content`, the inner one indented in regular `text-label`.
See <Story of={BandsStories.NestedGroups}/>.

Indent has the further advantage of keeping the columns aligned from the first
row to the last, which is the reason to group inside one table rather than break
it into separate sections.

## Two filled strips in a row

The column header and the first group caption are adjacent, because the first
group starts immediately under the header. Both are filled, and they are told
apart by type rather than by tint.

Taking the fill off the header to break the run is the wrong trade. It leaves a
header on the same ground as its own rows, and then the only thing marking it as
a header is the smallest text on the screen.

The run resolves on scroll in any case. The column header leaves and does not
come back; the caption is what pins to the top. Rows in two groups are
identical, so which group you are in is the thing a reader loses — and nobody
forgets what the columns were, because a date looks like a date and an amount
looks like an amount.

## Labels

Labels are `text-label`, not `text-muted`. A column header is read, not read
past. `--color-muted` is 2.6:1 on white, which is under the 4.5:1 that text this
size wants and under the 3:1 floor for large text; it belongs to text that is
there to be skipped, like the days either side of the month a calendar is
showing.

## Border to border

## Inside a card

For the case where the region genuinely is one object among several.

The card contributes the border, the radius and `overflow-hidden` — that last one
is what makes the top and bottom bands clip to the corners instead of squaring
them off. It contributes no padding: a band that stops short of the edge is a
row, and reads as one.

It also contributes the ground. `Card` is `bg-content-fg`, so the rows here are
white while the identical rows above are `content-bg`.

## Known gaps

`Card` ships `rounded` and a bare `border`. That is a smaller curve than the
radius scale intends and a border colour inherited from the text, so these
stories pass `rounded-surface border-content-border` on every card. The
component should carry them.

The fill is tuned against the ground rather than against white, because border
to border is the common case: 4.1 points below `content-bg` and 5.7 below
`content-fg`, so one value separates in both contexts.
